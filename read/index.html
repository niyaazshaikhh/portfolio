02 / Classification

Telling lawful
from unauthorised.

These are useful signals to check; they are not a substitute for title-by-title review.

SignalLawful sourcesUnauthorised sources
Right to hostPublic domain, open licence, author permission, or licensed lending.No permission from rights holders.
CatalogueOlder, openly licensed, or self-published titles.Current bestsellers and paid textbooks offered free.
Takedown responseStated rights-holder contact and process.Ignored, or answered with new domains.
StabilityOne established domain.Mirrors and frequent domain changes.

04 / Pattern

The takedown
cycle.

Reported distribution can move between domains and mirrors.

01

Site goes live

A catalogue appears under one domain.

02

Takedown

Notices, court orders, or domain seizures.

03

New address

The same catalogue appears on a new domain or mirror.

04

Re-indexed

Search and link sites list the new address.

Track the brand

The cycle repeats. Follow the brand and its mirrors, not one URL.

Access tiers

Free quotas with paid upgrades can leave account and payment trails.

05 / Response

Enforcement
routes.

Possible channels named in the monitoring brief.

01

Takedown notices

Notices to hosts, registrars, and CDNs under applicable copyright law.

02

Search delisting

Copyright removal requests to search engines for infringing URLs.

03

Payment and ad networks

Reports to processors and advertisers that fund or monetise the sites.

04

Litigation and law enforcement

The brief cites the 2022 US seizure of Z-Library domains and publisher suits against Sci-Hub.
